Top 5 Cyber Threats Facing Kenyan Businesses in 2026

Practical insights for SMEs and growing organisations across Kenya.

As digital adoption accelerates in Kenya, threat actors continue to refine their tactics. Here are the five most significant cyber threats we are seeing and expect to intensify through 2026.

1. Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS)

Ransomware groups now operate like professional software companies. Affiliates can rent sophisticated tools and target Kenyan organisations of all sizes. Double-extortion (encryption + data leak threats) remains common.

2. Sophisticated Phishing & Business Email Compromise (BEC)

Attackers increasingly use local context — M-Pesa references, KRA notices, supplier invoices, and WhatsApp messages — to trick employees into transferring funds or revealing credentials.

3. Cloud Misconfigurations & Account Takeovers

As more businesses move to Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace, poorly configured accounts and weak multi-factor authentication create easy entry points.

4. Supply-Chain & Third-Party Risks

Compromising a smaller supplier or service provider remains an effective way to reach larger targets. Many Kenyan SMEs are part of broader supply chains.

5. Insider Threats & Human Error

Accidental data exposure, weak password practices, and untrained staff continue to be among the most frequent root causes of incidents.

What You Can Do

Implement layered defences (endpoint protection, email security, monitoring), enforce multi-factor authentication, conduct regular awareness training, and have an incident response plan ready.
